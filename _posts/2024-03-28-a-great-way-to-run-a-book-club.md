<p>When I lived in Richardson, TX, I was invited to be part of a men’s book club. I was very impressed with how it was
  set up.</p>

<h2 id="the-setup">The setup</h2>

<p>We completed the following steps in a perpetual cycle</p>

<ol>
  <li>A host volunteers or is chosen.</li>
  <li>The host chooses three book candidates and the others vote* on which book to read.</li>
  <li>The host chooses three or four Thursday evenings and the others vote* to decide which one to meet on.</li>
  <li>The group reads the book.</li>
  <li>The host decides where to meet—generally at the local ice cream shop.</li>
  <li>The group meets to discuss the book.</li>
</ol>

<p>* We used <a href="https://web.groupme.com/signin">GroupMe</a> polls for these votes.</p>

<h2 id="what-i-liked-about-it">What I liked about it</h2>

<p>There’s a lot of genius to this setup. Since you’re voting on the book, you generally end up reading a book you’re at
  least somewhat interested in.</p>

<p>Because you’re voting on the date, the meeting is generally on a day when most people can make it and you can vote
  for more or less time depending on the length of the book and your own schedule.</p>

<p>Anyone can host regardless of their home situation since the ice cream shop is a default location. (Sometimes we met
  around a campfire in someone’s backyard, but most of the time we just went to <a
    href="https://www.braums.com/menu/">Braum’s</a>. Either way, it was a lot of fun and didn’t require much prep.)</p>

<p>Because the schedule of reads isn’t decided in advance, newcomers have a better chance at hosting and the group can
  adapt to new book releases.</p>
